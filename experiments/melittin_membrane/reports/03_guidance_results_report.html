BioEmu membrane guidance · report 3 of 3

Guidance works, consistently, and hits a ceiling

The Ez potential under the OPM reference frame improves every structure it touches, at every strength tested. The effect is real, highly significant, dose-dependent — and bounded by a fixed exchange rate between improvement and structural damage.

Headline. At λ = 20, guidance improves the membrane energy of 44 out of 44 structures (paired p = 4×10⁻⁸) at zero structural cost — physical validity 0.94, identical to baseline. Raising λ buys a larger per-structure effect and costs proportionally more broken structures, at a rate so constant that the total useful improvement per 50 samples never changes. That looks like a ceiling on position-only guidance, not a tuning problem.

What finally worked

The configuration is the unmodified Senes Ez potential evaluated on Cα atoms, with the membrane frame transferred by Kabsch superposition from the OPM-oriented melittin structure rather than searched for. Both halves matter, and neither was obvious:

  • Report 1 found that letting the objective choose its own membrane placement gave it an escape hatch — it could improve its score by moving the membrane rather than fixing the structure.
  • Report 2 fixed that, and found that ez_ca — which had looked mediocre under the broken frame — was in fact the best objective available, passing all three screens.

Ez under the OPM frame improves 100% of structures at every λ. Under the searched frame the same objective improves only 60–68% of them, which is what an exploitable objective looks like once the metric no longer shares the exploit.

The statistical error that hid it

Every significance test before this point was wrong, and the error ran in both directions. Runs in a sweep share base_seed, and generate_batch seeds each batch as base_seed + start_idx, so sample j of the guided run and sample j of the baseline start from the same prior draw. They are matched pairs. Testing them as independent samples throws away nearly all the signal.

same 44 pairs, same numerator (-0.1744), two denominators:

UNPAIRED SE = sqrt(s_g²/n + s_b²/n) = sqrt(0.9426/44 + 1.2137/44) = 0.2214
           t = -0.1744 / 0.2214 = -0.788              p = 0.43

PAIRED     SE = sd(d)/sqrt(n)         = 0.1742/sqrt(44)            = 0.0263
           t = -0.1744 / 0.0263 = -6.641             p = 4.3e-08

why sd(d) collapses:
  Var(g-b) = Var(g) + Var(b) - 2·rho·s_g·s_b
           = 0.9426 + 1.2137 - 2(0.9938)(0.9709)(1.1017) = 0.0303
  the variances sum to 2.16; the covariance term removes 2.13 of it.

ρ = 0.994 because guidance nudges each trajectory rather than producing an unrelated structure. It is the same move as a control variate, or evaluating two models on identical minibatches. The unpaired test asked "is a random guided structure better than a random baseline one?" — unanswerable at n = 44 when between-structure spread is 1.1 and the effect is 0.17. The paired test asks "when I guide this trajectory, does it improve?" Answer: every time.

This is only legitimate because the seeds match. A run with a different base_seed has no real pairing, and a paired test on it would be invalid.

There is no sweet spot — the exchange rate is fixed. Effect × yield, the total improvement surviving per 50 samples generated, stays between 0.141 and 0.160 across a 25× range of λ. The measured points sit on a constant-product contour. Turning λ up buys a larger per-structure effect and costs proportionally more structures, and the product does not move.

The one departure is at the very start. λ = 20 returns 0.029 of effect per structure lost, roughly twice the efficiency of anything above it; going from λ = 20 to λ = 35 costs seven more broken structures and buys only 0.017 more effect. λ = 20 is the operating point: nearly all the accessible effect, at the only structural cost near zero.

Why this reads as a ceiling

A constant exchange rate means every additional unit of guidance force splits the same way between improving the conformation and pulling the residue frames out of agreement. That is exactly what you would expect if the damage mechanism is intrinsic to the channel being guided.

BioEmu stores each residue as a Cα position plus a rotation, and reconstructs the backbone from both. Nothing enforces that C(i) and N(i+1) land 1.33 Å apart — the network has only learned to emit consistent consecutive frames. All of our guidance moves pos and never touches node_orientations, so the two channels drift apart and the reconstructed peptide bond is where it shows. The failure signature agrees: CN breaks dominate at every λ while clashes stay flat near baseline.

Keep the magnitude in perspective too. −0.174 kcal/mol is about 0.3 kT at room temperature: real, perfectly consistent, highly significant, and small.

What follows

  1. Guide the rotational channel. Rotating a residue's frame moves Cβ without moving any Cα, so it cannot stretch a bond and should not face this exchange rate at all. The λ curve makes that testable: if rotational guidance shows a different effect-versus-damage slope, the position channel was the binding constraint.
  2. Test peptides with more headroom. Melittin's unguided ensemble already sits near the limit a connected helix can reach. A transmembrane peptide, whose aqueous ensemble should differ sharply from its membrane state, gives guidance more to do.
  3. Re-validate the screen on noised intermediates. Every screen so far evaluated finished structures, but guidance acts on x̂₀ at intermediate noise. That gap is why the screen passed objectives that then failed in practice.
