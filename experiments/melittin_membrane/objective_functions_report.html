BioEmu membrane guidance · 650 structures

Which objectives can actually guide membrane insertion?

Nine candidate objectives, screened against generated melittin ensembles and an experimentally-oriented reference, before any of them is used for guidance. Two survive.

Headline. Seven of nine objectives can be improved by physically destroying the peptide — stretching the backbone or unfolding the helix. Only the two hydrophobic-moment objectives resist both. Separately, every objective that solves for its own membrane placement gets that placement badly wrong: predicted membrane normals sit 66–83° from the experimentally-determined one, essentially perpendicular to reality.

Why this screen exists

We guided BioEmu with the Ez depth potential and it appeared to work — energy improved significantly at high guidance strength. It was an artefact. Measured on the generated ensembles, Ez correlates positively with helicity (r = +0.52), and the structures that fail the physicality filter score better than those that pass. Guidance was lowering the energy by pulling the molecule apart.

That failure is structural, not a tuning problem. Ez is additive and per-residue, E = Σ f(type, depth), so nothing in it knows the residues are bonded. The cheapest way to lower it is to gain the freedom to place each residue at its own optimal depth — and both unfolding and chain-breaking buy exactly that freedom. The tests below are built to catch that whole class of failure cheaply, on structures we already had.

The tests

  1. T1 — helix correlation. Pearson r against DSSP helicity over 650 structures spanning 0–92% helix. Melittin binds membranes as an amphipathic helix, so a usable objective must score helical structures better: r < 0.
  2. T2 — stretch gaming. Scale every residue's depth 3× along the membrane normal. This separates residues exactly the way the Ez gradient wanted to, and breaks the chain doing it. A usable objective must get worse.
  3. T3 — expulsion gaming. Translate the whole peptide away from the bilayer. No objective should improve. (All nine passed; translation-invariant by construction.)
  4. T4 — reference rank. Percentile of the experimental structure within the generated ensemble.
  5. T5 — headroom. Gap between ensemble mean and best structure, in standard deviations. Near-zero means guidance has nothing to do.
  6. T6 — frame recovery. Given a structure already in its known membrane frame, does the objective find that frame? Ground truth from OPM: normal = z, midplane = 0.

The pattern is exactly the hypothesis. The only objectives that resist both tests are the two that couple residues — the hydrophobic moment is a vector sum over residues, so it needs their radial directions to stay coherent. Unfolding scrambles that coherence and stretching dilutes it, so neither can be used to cheat. Every purely additive per-residue objective, however carefully bounded, remains gameable.

Note that ez_clamped and ez_anchored were written specifically to close the escape routes, and both still fail. Bounding the reward was not enough; the problem is additivity itself.

The membrane frame is a separate failure

This one was not anticipated. Given the OPM-oriented melittin structure — where the membrane placement is already solved, with the midplane at z = 0 and the bilayer boundary at z = +15.4 Å — every objective that searches for its own frame picks a normal 66–83° away from the true one. Our tilt measurement reproduces OPM's published 65° tilt to within 1°, so the discrepancy is in the objectives, not the measurement.

This matters beyond the objectives themselves. All of our guidance runs used a per-structure optimised membrane frame, chosen specifically to avoid needing a reference structure. That choice now looks wrong: the optimiser finds whichever placement minimises a flawed objective, not the physical one. A reference-aligned frame — the Kabsch approach, given a correct melittin reference — is the more defensible option, and the OPM file now makes it available.

FAIL

Burial term places the peptide in the bilayer; moment term requires it to stay a folded amphipathic helix to collect the reward.

Ez + centroid anchor

FAIL

Ez plus a harmonic penalty keeping the peptide centroid at the midplane, blocking the 'push the whole peptide out of the membrane' solution.

Disqualified: improves by 1.62σ when the chain is stretched; predicts a membrane normal 82° from the true one.

Ez, per-residue reward clamped

FAIL

Ez with each residue's contribution clamped to [floor, 0]. Removes the gain from expelling a residue to infinity while keeping the Ez shape.

Disqualified: rewards loss of helix (r = +0.065); improves by 0.47σ when the chain is stretched; predicts a membrane normal 83° from the true one.

Amphipathic depth contrast

FAIL

Mean depth of polar minus mean depth of hydrophobic residues. A difference of means, so global translation is free and total expulsion scores zero.

Disqualified: rewards loss of helix (r = +0.175); improves by 9.15σ when the chain is stretched; predicts a membrane normal 66° from the true one.

Saturating hydrophobic burial

FAIL

Hydrophobicity-weighted smooth indicator of being inside the bilayer core. Bounded on both sides: no reward for infinite depth or infinite distance.

Disqualified: rewards loss of helix (r = +0.507); improves by 1.28σ when the chain is stretched; predicts a membrane normal 76° from the true one.

FAIL

Senes et al. 2007 depth potential on Ca atoms. The control: this is what we guided with, and it rewarded unfolding and chain-breaking.

What to do next

  1. Adopt a reference membrane frame. Use 2mlt_opm.pdb as the Kabsch target instead of solving for the frame. This removes a failure mode that affects every objective equally, and is independent of which objective wins.
  2. Build on the hydrophobic moment. It is the only term that survived, and it survived for a reason worth keeping: it couples residues. Combining it with a placement term needs care — combined_moment_heavy weights the moment 3× and is still gameable, because mixing a gameable term with a safe one leaves the mixture gameable.
  3. Re-validate before guiding. Any new objective goes through this screen first. It runs on existing structures in minutes with no GPU, and it would have caught the original Ez failure before four rounds of sampling.
  4. Reconsider the target peptide. Melittin's unguided ensemble already sits at the limit a connected helix can reach, so headroom is small whatever the objective.
