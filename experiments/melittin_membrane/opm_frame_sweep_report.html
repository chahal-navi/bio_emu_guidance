BioEmu membrane guidance · follow-up to the objective screen

The frame fix worked. The guidance still failed.

Replacing the searched membrane frame with one transferred from an experimentally-oriented structure repaired every objective on paper — then the guidance re-run destroyed 74–98% of structures anyway. This explains the gap.

In short. Transferring the membrane frame from the OPM melittin structure flipped all nine objectives to the correct sign on helicity and removed the stretch exploit. Guiding with the two best of them nonetheless collapsed physical validity from 0.94 to 0.02. The screen validated the objectives on BioEmu's finished structures, but guidance acts on noisy intermediates — and the failure that actually occurred is one no objective can fix, because it comes from guiding positions while leaving the rotational frames untouched.

What the OPM reference actually is

OPM (Orientations of Proteins in Membranes) takes a deposited structure and computes where it sits in an implicit bilayer, then republishes the coordinates already rotated and translated into that membrane frame. For 2mlt_opm.pdb the file states its geometry directly in the header:

REMARK      1/2 of bilayer thickness:   15.4

and carries 277 DUM pseudo-atoms, all at z = +15.400, marking the hydrocarbon boundary. The midplane is the z = 0 plane. Melittin's own Cα atoms span z = 1.76 to 20.09 Å, so it lies across the interface at a 65° tilt — a peripheral peptide resting on one leaflet, not a transmembrane one, which is why only a single boundary plane is drawn.

Is the membrane fixed?

Fixed relative to that one structure. The OPM file's membrane is the z = 0 plane in the OPM file's own coordinate system, and that is useless on its own, because BioEmu emits structures in arbitrary poses — the model is SE(3)-equivariant, so a generated structure has no canonical orientation to compare against.

So the membrane is transferred. Each generated structure is superimposed onto the OPM reference by Kabsch, which says how that structure would have to be rotated to match the reference's pose. We then carry the membrane plane back through that transformation and express it in the generated structure's own coordinates, as a normal n and an offset c, giving each residue a depth z = n·x − c. Mathematically this is identical to moving the structure into the OPM frame, but it leaves the coordinates untouched, which keeps the gradient clean. Verified exact: self-alignment returns n = (0, 0, 1) and c = 0, and a randomly rotated copy reproduces every residue depth to 0.0000 Å.

The searched frame gave the objective an escape hatch: for any conformation, however bad, it could find the membrane placement that scored best. "Improve the score" was therefore satisfiable by moving the membrane instead of improving the structure. A transferred frame is determined by shape-matching to the reference, which the objective has no influence over, so the only remaining way to improve the score is to change the conformation. That is why every correlation flipped.

The A/B, on 650 existing structures

Every objective improves. Helicity correlations go negative across the board — including ez_ca, the unmodified Ez potential we started with, at −0.559. The worst stretch exploit, depth_contrast at −9.05σ, becomes +0.46σ. ez_clamped also recovers its dynamic range (0.020 → 0.562): under the searched frame it could always find a placement that saturated its clamps.

Two bugs this exposed

  1. ez_ca silently ignored externally supplied frames. It delegated to EzMembranePotential.evaluate(), which always solves its own. Its first "OPM frame" result came back byte-identical to the searched one, which is what gave it away.
  2. moment_alignment had its sign inverted. With the OPM normal pointing away from the bilayer core, a hydrophobic face turned toward the core must give μ·n < 0. It had been rewarding the face pointing at solvent.

It failed at every λ. Physical validity falls from 0.94 to 0.24–0.26 at λ = 20 and to 0.02 at λ = 100 and above. combined did not improve its energy at all (−2.69 against the baseline's −2.68). moment_alignment reached −3.26, but with helicity 0.00 and one surviving structure — the original pathology exactly: energy down, molecule destroyed.

λ = 100 and λ = 500 differ by 0.001 nm in final coordinates, so the guidance clip saturates: above roughly λ = 100 the strength knob does nothing and only the direction matters.

Why the screen did not predict this

The screen asked whether stretching a realistic, finished BioEmu structure improves the score. It does not — that result stands. But guidance never touches finished structures. It acts on the Tweedie estimate x̂₀ at intermediate noise levels, where x̂₀ is not a realistic peptide at all. An objective's behaviour on the final distribution says nothing about its behaviour on noisy intermediates. The screen tested the wrong distribution.

What the failure signature says

Look at which test fails: CN breaks at 0.74–0.98, clashes only 0.04–0.10. Peptide bonds, not steric overlap. That points somewhere specific.

BioEmu stores each residue as a rigid body — a Cα position plus a rotation matrix — and reconstructs N, C, O and Cβ from that pair. Nothing enforces that C(i) and N(i+1) land 1.33 Å apart; the network has merely learned to emit consecutive frames whose reconstructed atoms form proper bonds. All of our guidance pushes pos and never touches node_orientations, so the two channels drift out of agreement, and the reconstructed peptide bond is exactly where that disagreement becomes visible. No choice of objective repairs this, because it is not about the objective. It is about which channel is being guided.

What follows

  1. Guide the rotational channel. Rotating a residue's frame moves Cβ without moving any Cα, so it structurally cannot stretch a bond. This is the one approach immune to the failure that has now killed every run. rot_guidance_unverified.py is the starting point, and moment_alignment is already orientation-dependent, which such guidance requires.
  2. Re-validate on noised intermediates. The screen is salvageable: add x̂₀ estimates at several noise levels to its test set. That is a cheap repair to a methodology error, and it would have predicted this failure.
  3. Put the physicality terms back on. They measurably helped before (maximum stretch 16.3 Å → 6.4 Å), and there is no longer any reason to run without them.
